Caring for your weaving

Your basket will keep changing for a little while after you take it home, and that is part of its life. If we worked with rehydrated materials, keep it somewhere with good airflow while it finishes drying. That takes a week or two, or just a couple of hours if it was made from soft, leafy materials. If we worked with fresh material, it will shrink a little and settle into its shape as it dries over the next two or three weeks.

After that, there is very little to do. About once a year, give it a gentle spray with the hose to rinse off the dust, and let it dry somewhere with good airflow. That is really all it needs.

Sometimes accidents happen. If you ever notice mould starting, mix one tablespoon of bleach into five gallons of water and let the basket soak for ten minutes, no longer. Then set it somewhere warm with good airflow to dry. I like to use the heat vent in my house.

And if your first baskets lean or wobble, keep them close. Those are the ones every weaver treasures most, the ones that hold the learning in their shape.
