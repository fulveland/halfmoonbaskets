A Weekend to Slow Down

Weave, walk, and gather at the table

Over three days at Folk Tree Lodge, we weave a willow basket from base to border, walk the land to meet the willow where it grows, and gather for slow, plant-based meals made by Roni Zaide of Roni's Kitchen. Mornings begin gently with qigong by the fire. Come for the full weekend, or join us for a single day of weaving.

Arrive Friday and go straight to weaving. We put willow in your hands soon after you arrive, and from there the days move at the pace of your hands. On Friday you can choose a 10 in the morning or a noon start, and our first meal together is dinner, so bring a lunch and snacks for the afternoon. You will settle into your room later in the day, once the weaving is done.

Over the weekend you will weave a twisted willow basket from base to border, working with an abundance of willow toward a generously sized basket with real movement and character. We will walk out to meet the willow where it grows, gather at the table for meals made by Roni, and open each morning with gentle qigong by the fire.

Skills learned

  • Tying a slath base
  • Pairing
  • Waling
  • Rope coil weave
  • A rod border
  • An optional wrapped core handle

About the basket

This project introduces a few intermediate techniques. Some weaving experience helps, but beginners with patience and curiosity are warmly welcome, and the rope coil weave is forgiving, so each basket takes on its own character. Everyone finishes at least a small bowl, with optional free-weave time to take a larger basket further, up to roughly two feet across.

Day Retreat · Beginner willow

The Day Retreat

$349

Saturday, October 17 12 PM–8 PM Register

If a full weekend is more than you can step away for, join us for the day. Arrive at noon for lunch, then walk out in the afternoon to learn how to find and identify willow where it grows.

Back at the lodge, you will weave a small willow berry boat, a shallow tray made for gathering. We close the day with dinner and a fire. Two nourishing meals are included, and all tools and materials are provided.

Skills learned

  • Finding, identifying, and harvesting willow responsibly
  • Single rod randing, also called single strand randing
  • Choosing willow for the different parts of the basket
  • Shaping with a jig
  • Working willow so it bends without cracking

Who You'll Weave With

Your hosts

Freyja Ulveland

Half Moon Baskets

Freyja is the weaver behind Half Moon Baskets, based in Red Deer. She learned alongside the many generous women who have tended Alberta's basketry community for decades, gaining not only technique but a deep sense of care and connection. She works with willow, cattail, bulrush, and bark gathered close to home, and at the retreat she guides the weaving, the willow walk, and the quiet rhythm of the days.

Roni Zaide

Roni's Kitchen

Roni is a food storyteller, a kitchen witch, cookbook author, and cooking instructor at Roni's Kitchen. She cooks the nutrient-dense, plant-based meals that hold the weekend together, and shares the stories behind them. Her food is generous, seasonal, and made to be shared, a good part of why this weekend feels like a retreat and not just a workshop.

Before You Book

Questions weavers ask

Do I need weaving experience?

No. The weekend basket introduces a few intermediate techniques, but beginners with patience and curiosity are warmly welcome, and the rope coil weave is forgiving. The day retreat is a gentle beginner project.

Can I come for just the day?

Yes. The Day Retreat on Saturday is a single day of weaving, a willow walk, and two shared meals, with no overnight stay.

Can I join without staying overnight?

Yes. The Locals Special includes all of the weekend's meals and activities without a room, for those who live nearby or would rather find their own stay.

Can I share a room with a friend?

Shared rooms hold two people, and the Private Room for Two is a queen room booked for a pair. Let us know who you are coming with when you register.

How far is it from Calgary?

Folk Tree Lodge sits in the foothills near Bragg Creek, roughly a 30 to 50 minute drive from Calgary, depending on where in the city you start.

What are the meals like?

All meals are plant-based, made by Roni of Roni's Kitchen, with gluten-free and nut-free options. If you have allergies or particular needs, email Roni at info@roniskitchen.com before you book.

What if I can't make it after I register?

Because meals, materials, and rooms are arranged well ahead, tickets are non-refundable. You are warmly welcome to pass your place to someone else.

What to Bring

  • All weaving tools and materials are provided. Bring your own tools too, if you have them.
  • Indoor shoes or slippers, and comfortable clothing for hands-on making.
  • Warm layers, a jacket, and boots for the willow walk, in case we kneel to harvest.
  • A bag lunch, snacks, and water for Friday afternoon, since our first shared meal is dinner.
  • A water bottle and any supports your hands or body need for a full day.
  • An overnight bag, if you are staying at the lodge.

We weave in a heated quonset, though the temperature can shift with the weather, so layers are wise.

Getting There

Folk Tree Lodge, Bragg Creek, Alberta, about 30 to 50 minutes west of Calgary depending on where you start. Heading south from the junction of Highway 762 and 22X, it is the second driveway on the left, marked by a small Folk Tree Lodge sign.
