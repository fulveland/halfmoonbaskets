About the Weekend

Basketry is older than pottery. Older than metalwork. For thousands of years, people have been working with plants in their hands. Bending, twisting, weaving vessels to carry and hold.

These workshops teach the same foundational skills, with materials that grow where we live.

Location & Practical Details

Pioneer Lodge in Red Deer

Workshops take place at Pioneer Lodge, a historic community hall in Parkvale, next to the arena and curling rink.

The lodge is wheelchair accessible, has off-street parking, and includes a kitchen with a fridge and microwave.

Finding your own rhythm

A note for the workshops with guided instruction. After the guided portion, you'll have time to weave on your own and bring your basket to a finish. This stretch is meant to feel unhurried. Here's what to know going in.

There's no single right pace

Some hands move quickly, some settle in slowly, and both make lovely baskets. If the person beside you is further along, it doesn't mean you're behind. It means you're weaving at your own speed, which is exactly what this time is for.

Getting stuck is part of weaving

Natural materials have their own character, and every weaver hits a moment where something won't sit right. When it happens, set your work down and take a breath. A loosened row or an awkward join is almost always fixable. Try the last step once more, slowly. Often the material just needs a little more coaxing. And if it still won't come, wave me over.

You won't be forgotten

I'll be moving through the room throughout. If I'm mid-lesson with another group when you need me, flag me anyway. I'll catch your eye and come to you as soon as I've set them going.

Finishing at home is welcome

Some baskets come together within the session. Others want a little more time. If you'd rather carry yours home to finish, you'll leave knowing how, and you're always welcome to email a photo if you get stuck partway.

A few things that help

Keep your material in good working condition as you were shown, so it stays supple and easy to work. Tuck your ends in as you go rather than saving them for the end. And ask questions the moment they arise. There's no such thing as a small one here.

Mostly, settle in and let your hands find the rhythm. That's the whole point of this quiet stretch.
