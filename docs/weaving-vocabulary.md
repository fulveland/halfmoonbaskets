# Weaving vocabulary — willow, cattail, bulrush

A working reference of real craft terms, organized by material. Built so copy can use specific, grounded language ("stakes," "randing," "mellowing") instead of generic "natural materials" phrasing. Worth checking against the actual house teaching vocabulary before publishing — some terms vary by tradition/instructor.

## Willow (osier)

**Material terms**
- rod / withy / osier / whip — a single willow stem prepared for weaving
- butt (thick, cut end) vs. tip (thin, growing end)
- brown willow — bark left on, natural
- white willow — bark stripped while green, pale
- buff willow — boiled with bark on, then stripped; warm tan color
- steamed willow — buff turned deeper red-brown by steam heat
- osier bed / withy bed — a field grown specifically for basketry rods

**Structure terms**
- stake — an upright, structural rod that the weavers pass through
- weaver — the rod being woven horizontally through the stakes
- slath — the crossed sticks that start a round base
- upsett / upsetting — bending the stakes upward to begin the sides
- bye-stake — an extra stake added partway through for fullness
- border — the finished top edge (trac border, plait border, rope border are named styles)

**Technique terms**
- randing — simple over-one, under-one weave
- pairing — two weavers twisted around each other as they pass through the stakes
- waling — three or more rods worked together in a twisted band, used for strength at base and rim
- slewing — several weavers carried together as one, for faster coverage
- French randing — each stake gets its own weaver, all worked at once around the basket
- scallom / scollop cut — the sloped cut used to splice one rod into another

**Prep terms**
- soaking — submerging dried rods until pliable
- mellowing — wrapping soaked rods in damp cloth so moisture evens out before weaving
- kink — a controlled bend point, not a flaw

## Cattail (Typha)

- blade / leaf — the flat cattail leaf, the main working material
- cattail down — the soft seed-head fluff, historically used for stuffing/insulation, not structural weaving
- splitting — dividing a leaf lengthwise for finer, more flexible strands
- dampening / misting — keeping cut leaves workable while weaving; cattail dries and stiffens fast
- plaiting — flat, braid-like weaving, common for cattail mats
- twining — two strands twisted around each other, trapping the uprights between twists; the standard cattail basket/mat technique

## Bulrush / tule (Schoenoplectus)

- culm — the round, pithy stem of a rush; the basic unit of bulrush weaving (equivalent of willow's "rod")
- tule — common name for certain bulrush species, long used by Pacific Northwest and California basket weavers for mats and boats
- pith — the light, spongy interior of the stem
- curing — drying cut stems before storage
- rehydrating — soaking cured stems to bring back flexibility before working
- bundle / foundation — a gathered group of stems used as the core in coiled work
- stitching element — the thinner material wrapped around a bundle to bind it, in coiling
- random weave — loose, irregular weaving with no fixed repeat, mixing stem thicknesses freely (already the name of one Half Moon Baskets workshop)

## Shared vocabulary (applies across materials)

- coil — a technique building up a spiral form, bundle wrapped and stitched round after round
- twine / twining — two strands twisted around each other as they pass the uprights
- splice / join — connecting a new piece as the working strand runs out
- tension — how tightly the weave is pulled; controls final shape
- grain — the natural fiber direction; matters when splitting material cleanly
- green (freshly cut, still flexible) vs. cured/dried (needs rehydrating before use)

## Sensory / descriptive words (for copy, not just technique)

damp, pliant, springy, fibrous, woody, glossy (buff willow specifically), grassy, earthy, cool underwater, the dry rustle of cured leaves, the snap of a rod that's too dry, bend, tuck, twist, spiral, bundle, strand, blade, stem, whip.
